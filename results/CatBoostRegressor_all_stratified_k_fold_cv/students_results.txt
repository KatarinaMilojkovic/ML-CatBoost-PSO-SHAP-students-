============ train_test_split... ============
Why stratification of the target for train/test split?
--- Split will be 80/20 but that does not guarantee that the target grade distribution will be the same in both sets.
--- Stratification ensures that the target grade distribution is preserved in both training and test sets.
--- In other words, it will not happen that the test set has all representations of one grade and the training set has 0.
--- Stratification uses the ordinal grade values 0..7 as discrete labels (the target itself stays numeric for the regressor).
--- --- The train_test_split makes a representative 80/20 train/test split.
--- --- That representative train set is used by the fitness function to make representative 3 folds.
--- --- That representative train set is used by the optimized model.
training set: 116 students | test set: 29 students


============ Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)... ============
For hyperparameter optimization, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is used to identify an optimal learning rate, depth, L2 regularization, and random_strength.
--- Imagine it like this: PSO optimizer randomly generates particles within defined bounds.
--- Here, the pso optimizer is implemented using the Global Best topology ('global best pso optimizer').
--- Global best influences particles movement -> particles are influenced to move toward the global-best particle (particle with the best fitnesse).
--- The number of particles(10) and iterations(15) was chosen to balance optimization quality and computational efficiency.
PSO optimizer proposes particles (hyperparameter combinations) and calls a custom fitness function to evaluate them.
Here, the fitness function evaluates all particles in parallel using temporary CatBoostRegressor model (RMSE) with 3-fold stratified cross-validation on thetraining set only (80%).
--- Why CatBoostRegressor?
--- --- Because GRADE is an ordinal scale: predicting 6 when the truth is 7 is a smaller error than predicting 0. Regression (RMSE) respects that order, multiclass classification does not.
--- Why k-fold stratified cross-validation strategy?
--- --- To get a more reliable particle evaluation score (splitting the training set into 3 folds)
--- --- Why spliting is better?
--- --- --- Because splitting once, the score can depend on which students happen to be in the test set.
--- --- --- When it is split 3 times, the score is more reliable.
--- --- Why it has shuffle enabled?
--- --- --- Because the folds could be affected by the original ordering of the data.
--- --- --- The data will be shuffled before the folds are created
--- --- StratifiedKFold creates folds while maintaining approximately the same grade distribution in each fold (like the strytify in train_test_split).
--- --- Why 3-fold?
--- --- --- The 3-fold setup provided a balance between computational efficiency and statistical reliability.
--- Why only on training set?
--- --- Because the test set is reserved for final evaluation and should not be used during hyperparameter optimization.
--- --- The training set is split into 3 folds and the fitness function evaluation score is based on the train/test set in that split.
The fitness function calculates the RMSE for each fold and returns the mean RMSE to PSO.
The goal is to find the particle that gives the lowest evaluation score (mean RMSE across 3 folds).
Why?
--- RMSE is the typical size of the grade error (in grade points, big errors are punished more).
--- The model needs to predict grades close to the real ones for all students.
--- Students are weighted by their grade's balanced class weight during training, so rare grades are not ignored.
Important thing to note: PSO minimizes the objective/fitness function.
--- RMSE is already 'lower is better', so (unlike Macro F1 in the classifier version) it is NOT negated.
A fixed random seed (42) was used to ensure reproducibility throughout hyperparameter evaluations.
