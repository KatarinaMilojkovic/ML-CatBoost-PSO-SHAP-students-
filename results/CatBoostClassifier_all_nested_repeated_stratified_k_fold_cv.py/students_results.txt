============ Step 1: train_test_split (80% / 20%)... ============
Why stratification of the target for train/test split?
--- Split will be 80/20 but that does not guarantee that the target class distribution will be the same in both sets.
--- Stratification ensures that the target class distribution is preserved in both training and test sets.
--- In other words, it will not happen that the test set has all representations of one class and the training set has 0.
--- --- The train_test_split makes a representative 80/20 train/test split.
--- --- The 80% training set is used for the entire nested cross-validation procedure (Step 2) and the final ensemble (Step 3).
--- --- The 20% test set is touched only once, at the very end of Step 3, for the final evaluation.
training set: 116 students | test set: 29 students


============ Step 2: Nested cross-validation for hyperparameter search (15 outer splits) ============
Why nested cross-validation instead of a single PSO run on the 80% training set?
--- A single PSO run picks hyperparameters using one specific inner cross-validation, and we never
--- see how well THOSE specific hyperparameters would have generalized to data they never influenced at all.
--- Nested CV fixes this: the 80% training set is itself split into an OUTER training portion and an
--- OUTER validation portion, 15 times, with 15 different random partitions ('outer stratified splits').
--- For each of the 15 outer splits:
--- --- INNER step: PSO searches for hyperparameters using ONLY the outer-training portion, with a
--- --- 3-fold REPEATED stratified cross-validation (3 folds x 2 repeats = 6 evaluations per particle,
--- --- with class_weights) as the fitness function -- exactly the same fitness function design used
--- --- for a single, non-nested PSO run in the earlier version of this script.
--- --- OUTER step: a model trained with those hyperparameters on the outer-training portion is
--- --- evaluated on the OUTER validation portion, which the inner PSO search never saw.
--- Because the outer validation portion is different, unseen data in every one of the 15 repeats,
--- the 15 outer-validation scores give an (almost) unbiased estimate of how well this whole
--- pipeline (PSO + CatBoost) generalizes -- reported below as mean +/- std and a 95% CI.
--- After all 15 outer splits are done, the 15 separate hyperparameter sets found by the 15 inner
--- PSO searches are combined (median of each hyperparameter) into ONE final set, used in Step 3.
N_OUTER_SPLITS=2, PSO_N_PARTICLES=10, PSO_N_ITERS=15 (see the cost warning in the file docstring)

============ Step 3: final ensemble -- 5 x (3-fold repeated stratified CV), trained on 80%, tested on 20% ============
Using the final hyperparameters from Step 2, 5 independent 'replicates' are trained.
--- Each replicate runs its own 3-fold REPEATED stratified cross-validation (3 folds x 2 repeats
--- = 6 fold-models, with class_weights) over the WHOLE 80% training set: every fold-model is
--- trained on that fold's training portion (with the held-out fold as eval_set for early
--- stopping) and then predicts on the untouched 20% test set.
--- The 6 fold-models of one replicate are soft-voted (averaged) into that replicate's prediction.
--- The 5 replicates are then soft-voted together -- 30 CatBoost models in total contribute to
--- the final prediction, which is exactly the kind of full-ensemble explanation SHAP uses below.
