============ Step 1: train_test_split (80% / 20%)... ============
Why stratification of the target for train/test split?
--- Split will be 80/20 but that does not guarantee that the target class distribution will be the same in both sets.
--- Stratification ensures that the target class distribution is preserved in both training and test sets.
--- In other words, it will not happen that the test set has all representations of one class and the training set has 0.
--- --- The train_test_split makes a representative 80/20 train/test split.
--- --- The 80% training set is used for the PSO hyperparameter search (Step 2) and the final ensemble (Step 3).
--- --- The 20% test set is touched only once, at the very end of Step 3, for the final evaluation.
training set: 116 students | test set: 29 students


============ Step 2: PSO hyperparameter search on the 80% train set (3-fold repeated stratified CV fitness) ============
For hyperparameter optimization, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is used to identify an optimal learning rate, depth, L2 regularization, and random_strength.
--- Imagine it like this: PSO optimizer randomly generates particles within defined bounds.
--- Here, the pso optimizer is implemented using the Global Best topology ('global best pso optimizer').
--- Global best influences particles movement -> particles are influenced to move toward the global-best particle (particle with the bestfitnesse).
--- The number of particles(10) and iterations(15) was chosen to balance optimization quality and computational efficiency.
PSO optimizer proposes particles (hyperparameter combinations) and calls a custom fitness function to evaluate them.
Here, the fitness function evaluates all particles in parallel using a temporary CatBoostClassifier with 3-fold REPEATED stratified cross-validation (with class_weights) on the training set only (80%).
--- Why CatBoostClassifier?
--- --- Because it is used for multiclass classification problems
--- Why k-fold stratified cross-validation strategy?
--- --- To get a more reliable particle evaluation score (splitting the training set into 3 folds)
--- --- Why spliting is better?
--- --- --- Because splitting once, the score can depend on which students happen to be in the test set.
--- --- --- When it is split 3 times, the score is more reliable.
--- --- Why it has shuffle enabled?
--- --- --- Because the folds could be affected by the original ordering of the data.
--- --- --- The data will be shuffled before the folds are created
--- --- StratifiedKFold creates folds while maintaining approximately the same class distribution in each fold (like the strytify in train_test_split).
--- --- Why 3-fold?
--- --- --- The 3-fold setup provided a balance between computational efficiency and statistical reliability.
--- Why REPEATED k-fold stratified cross-validation strategy?
--- --- 3 folds x 3 repeats = 9 evaluations per particle give a more stable fitness score than a single 3-fold pass,
--- --- because splitting once means the score can depend heavily on which students land in which fold.
--- Why only on the training set?
--- --- Because the test set is reserved for final evaluation and should not be used during hyperparameter optimization.
--- --- The training set is split into 3 folds and the fitness function evaluation score is based on the train/test set in that split.
The fitness function calculates the Macro F1 score for each of the 9 folds and returns the negative mean Macro F1 score to PSO.
The goal is to find the particle that gives the highest evaluation score (mean Macro F1 score across the 9 folds).
Why?
--- F1 score measures how well the model predicts one class.
--- Macro F1 score measures how well the model predicts each class (average of F1 scores across all classes)
--- The model needs to make good predictions for all classes, not just the majority class.
--- That's why we need to find a particle that gives the highest average Macro F1 score!
--- Macro F1 score tells us about the model's balanced performance across all classes, which is important for imbalanced datasets like this one.
Important thing to note: PSO minimizes the objective/fitness function (in other words, PSO will choose the lowest Macro F1 score returnedby the fitness function).
This is why the evaluation score (Macro F1 score) returned to PSO is negated.
--- So, when PSO chooses the lowest negative Macro F1 score its actually choosing the highest positive Macro F1 score.
--- in other words, by minimizing the negative Macro F1 score, PSO is maximizing the positive Macro F1 score.
A fixed random seed (42) was used to ensure reproducibility throughout hyperparameter evaluations.

============ Step 3: final ensemble -- 5 x (3-fold repeated stratified CV), trained on 80%, tested on 20% ============
Using the final hyperparameters from Step 2, 5 independent 'replicates' are trained.
--- Each replicate runs its own 3-fold REPEATED stratified cross-validation (3 folds x 3 repeats
--- = 9 fold-models, with class_weights) over the WHOLE 80% training set: every fold-model is
--- trained on that fold's training portion (with the held-out fold as eval_set for early
--- stopping) and then predicts on the untouched 20% test set.
--- The 9 fold-models of one replicate are soft-voted (averaged) into that replicate's prediction.
--- The 5 replicates are then soft-voted together -- 45 CatBoost models in total contribute to
--- the final prediction, which is exactly the kind of full-ensemble explanation SHAP uses below.
